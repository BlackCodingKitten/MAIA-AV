\section{Preliminar Analysis Result}
\subsection*{Expected Behaviour under the Preliminar Condition}
Prior to examining the experimental outcomes, qualitative expectations are delineated regarding model performance under the frame-only condition, providing a methodological framework for interpreting distinctions across question categories without retrospectively deriving explanations from observed results.
For questions related to spatial dimensions, it is anticipated that there will be a relatively high presence of visual evidence, due to the inherent visibility of entity locations, movements, object configurations, and spatial relationships within the visual stream. The complexity may increase, however, when it becomes necessary to reconstruct this information over multiple temporal intervals, such as when tracking an entity through a series of movements or elucidating a spatial configuration resulting from several sequential actions.
Temporal questions are expected to display a more varied profile. Actions and event sequences that are visually distinctive can often be pinpointed through timestamp data and organized by the temporal structure produced during Event Analysis. However, not all temporal information may be fully recoverable from visual input alone. Some questions might involve events where interpretation is reliant on a linguistic context or on interrelations between moments that remain ambiguous when only visual information is available. The adequacy of the resulting representation thus hinges on both the quality of chronological reconstruction and the inherent characteristics of the events involved.
Causal questions are anticipated to be the most challenging within the visual-only scenario. Although the visual stream can provide insights into event succession, interactions, and observable state alterations, the underlying reasons for these events may rely on spoken dialogue or remain indistinct from mere images. Motivations, intentions, and explanatory relationships are less likely to be directly supported by visual evidence on its own. This supposition also emphasizes the methodological distinction between temporal succession and causal interdependence, as the occurrence of one event preceding another should not be interpreted as evidence of causation alone.
These expectations are crafted independently of empirical performance and serve exclusively as a qualitative reference for further investigation. There is no presumption concerning the degree of category-level differences or which category might eventually attain the highest proficiency scores.
Subsequent to this, proficiency results are contrasted with downstream Visual Semantic Verification (VSV) performance in order to differentiate between evidence availability and task execution. Visual proficiency determines if the independently constructed representation contains sufficient information to distinguish between corresponding captions and their counterparts (foils). In contrast, VSV performance evaluates whether the model delivers the correct response when both the question and inputs specific to the experimental condition are directly accessible.
Together, these two metrics provide a diagnostic perspective that surpasses task accuracy alone. A Pass proficiency result paired with a correct VSV response indicates a scenario where relevant visual evidence was both represented and successfully applied in the downstream task. Conversely, a Pass outcome linked to an incorrect response suggests that despite having adequate evidence in the preliminary representation, there was failure in the downstream task. Such a scenario points to potential issues with evidence selection, integration, or reasoning at a later stage.
When both proficiency and VSV performance are negative, it suggests an inadequacy at the perceptual or representational level, as neither the preliminary representation provides sufficient evidence for proficiency nor does the downstream task succeed. However, this comparison alone does not pinpoint the exact source of failure.
The most diagnostically informative configuration emerges when proficiency indicates Not Pass but the downstream VSV response is accurate. In such cases, correct answer selection occurs despite lacking a visual representation that meets predefined proficiency criteria. This could be attributed to utilizing partial cues, alternative evidence, parametric priors, shortcut strategies, or mere guessing; however, the comparison alone does not identify the specific mechanism responsible for deriving the answer.
A joint analysis of visual proficiency and downstream performance thus enables differentiation of whether the requisite evidence for a question was already present before question exposure or whether the model ultimately executed the task successfully. This offers a richer diagnostic interpretation of VSV performance compared to assessing accuracy in isolation.
\subsection*{Early Observations and Interpretation}
As discussed in Section \ref{sec:pa}, the preliminary analysis serves to determine whether the visual content of a video alone allows the model to answer questions correctly. Crucially, it also acts as a diagnostic tool to verify that the newly created questions do not share the same limitation as MAIA \cite{testa2025maia} namely, a primary reliance on visual content that prevents a genuine assessment of cross-modal information integration.
This analysis focuses on an earlier stage of the "\textit{cognitive process}", specifically the semantic construction of observed content. Through this approach, it is possible to verify whether models can independently extract the required information from video frames, thereby distinguishing knowledge genuinely grounded in visual evidence from responses influenced by linguistic priors.
The obtained results partially confirm the expected hierarchy among different levels of visual understanding, showing that the difficulty of extracting task-relevant information increases when moving from direct perception toward higher-level semantic interpretation. 
As anticipated, the analysis reveals that entity-level information is the most accessible from visual evidence, as the model only needs to establish a correspondence between visual elements and their semantic representations, mapping perceptual inputs to linguistic concepts. The results obtained from the two larger models confirm the expectation of a direct grounding mechanism between the visual signal and semantic entities. Qwen-30B and Gemma-12B achieve the highest proficiency values in entity-related information, with an \textbf{average entity-recovery score} of 34.47 and 33.77, respectively. This suggests that larger models possess a stronger ability to associate visual patterns with their corresponding conceptual categories.
The smaller models present a more nuanced picture. While the same relative hierarchy is preserved, the absolute ability to recover visually grounded entities changes substantially across model families. For the smaller Gemma-E4B, the \textbf{average entity-recovery score} is 27.87, which is lower than that of Gemma-12B but still clearly above its own event and inference scores.
